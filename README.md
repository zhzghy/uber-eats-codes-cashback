# Uber Eats promo code: how to find codes that actually work, stack a second discount, and claim the cashback most people leave behind

You type a code into the checkout box, hit apply, and nothing happens. No error message, no explanation — the order total doesn't move. That's the normal experience with most Uber Eats promo codes floating around the internet, and it's not a bug in your app.

The codes are usually real. They just weren't meant for you.

## Why so many "working" promo codes don't work for you

Most of what gets shared as an Uber Eats promo code is a referral code that someone extracted from their own account and posted publicly. Uber's own help documentation describes how these work: you find your personal invite code in your account info, share it, and when somebody places their first Uber Eats order with it, *you* get a promo to use on your current or next order.

Read that again. The reward goes to the person who owns the code, not necessarily the person typing it. Some invite codes do carry a benefit for the new user too, but the structure is referral-first, which is why a code that "worked for someone two hours ago" can fail on your account. It may be region-locked, it may be for first-time users only, or it may have already hit its usage cap.

Then there's the expiry problem. Plenty of published codes are tied to a partner launch. Stop & Shop's 40%-off first-order code went out with a hard cut-off of 31 May 2026 — the code page still gets indexed long after the discount is dead.

## The rules Uber Eats actually enforces

These come from Uber's help centre and the official promo page, and they explain most of the frustrating cases:

- **One code per order.** You cannot stack two promo codes. If the code is for a fixed dollar amount, any unused portion doesn't roll over to your next order.
- **Apply before ordering.** Codes can't be added after the order is placed.
- **Store promos apply first, then your highest-value account promo.** You can manually pick a specific code for a specific order, or select none and save it for later.
- **Some codes are geographic.** City, state or country restrictions are common.
- **Some codes only arrive by email or in-app notification.** For those, you have to order from the account whose email matches the one that received the code. Grab a code from a blog post and it will fail this check.
- **Rides promos don't work on delivery or pickup orders.**
- **Promo codes do combine with Uber Cash and other discounts.** That part is genuinely flexible — the single-code limit is about codes specifically.

That last point matters more than it looks. It means the real question isn't "how do I stack two codes" — you can't — but "what else can sit on top of the code I already have."

## Codes that come from the brands themselves

Partner codes are the most reliable category because they're published by the restaurant or retailer, not scraped from a forum. They also expire on a stated date, which makes them easy to sanity-check.

**McDonald's** publishes a first-order code directly on its delivery page: `mcds2026`, $5 off a minimum $20 order, US only, valid through 31 December 2026. Terms state one order per customer, first order only, must be applied in the app before completing checkout, and it cannot be combined with other offers. Odd code for a 2026 promo, but that's what's on the page.

**Aldi's** launch code `ALDIUBER25` took 40% off Aldi orders of $30 or more, up to $25 in savings, when Aldi joined the platform. That launched in September 2025, so verify it before planning a grocery run around it.

**Uber Eats Fresh Days** is a recurring one rather than a code: Uber One members get 30% off selected produce, meat, dairy and eggs from Aldi and other retailers every Tuesday.

**Students** have two separate routes. Student Beans lists $10 off your first three orders in the US (minimum $10 basket, first-time users only; the Australian version is $10 off a $30+ first order). Separately, Uber One for Students is running 12 months of membership free, while supplies last or through 21 October 2026 — you need to be an eligible student, 18+, not already an active Uber One member.

If you're an existing customer wondering where your codes went: they're mostly in your account already. Uber's own FAQ says available promotions may be listed inside your account, and individual restaurants sometimes attach discounts for customers who hit a spend threshold. Check the home screen and your saved restaurants rather than a coupon site.

## What aggregator sites are listing right now

Coupon aggregators do list current codes, and Wethrift's Uber Eats page is one of the more transparent examples — it timestamps when each code was last reported working and how many times it's been used.

As of the snapshot captured for its June 2026 listing, its top code was `LOURDES15` ($15 off a first order of $20+, 656 total uses), followed by `UBEREATS` ($20 off your next order), `EATS25NOW` ($25 off) and `ORDER50` (50% off). The page claimed 30 active codes in total.

Worth being blunt about these: several are obviously first-name-plus-discount patterns, which is the signature of a personal referral code, not a public campaign. "Reported working 2 hours ago by a shopper" tells you it worked on *that* account. On yours, the same code may fail the new-user check, the region check, or the email-match check.

Treat aggregator codes as a scratchcard. Try one, don't plan your budget around it.

## Why your cashback didn't track

ShopBack's own guide on food delivery cashback admits the category "has the highest tracking failure rate" on the platform, and it names the causes. If you're going to bother, know the failure modes:

1. **The session window.** Uber Eats requires the order within 2 hours of your click-through. Click at noon, order at 6pm, no cashback.
2. **Device switching.** Clicking through on a laptop and then ordering in the phone app breaks the session. Same device, same session, start to finish.
3. **Cart contents.** Items already sitting in your Uber Eats cart before you click through from the cashback platform aren't tracked. Empty the cart first.
4. **Ad-blocking.** Ad blockers, third-party extensions and other cashback or loyalty plugins can kill the tracking cookie. Uber Eats also needs its cookies accepted for the order to be confirmed.
5. **Mobile privacy settings.** ShopBack's guide specifically notes that iOS "Limit Ad Tracking" and the Android equivalent can prevent orders from tracking.
6. **Cancellations and refunds.** Rejected, cancelled or returned orders earn nothing.

Cashback typically shows as pending within a couple of days and confirms after roughly 45 days for Uber Eats — faster than most categories, since the platform only holds it until the refund window closes.

There's also a distinction worth watching. Australian deal-forum threads have documented cases where a promised new-user cashback landed in a restricted "Online Bonus" balance rather than as withdrawable cash. If a bonus arrives, check which bucket it went into before you count it.

## The one rule that trips people up

This is the load-bearing detail, and it's in the terms of essentially every cashback page for Uber Eats:

> Cashback is not valid with vouchers or promo codes that aren't featured on the cashback platform.

So you're choosing between paths, not stacking them. Apply a random blog code at checkout and you can void the cashback on that order. For a $15-off code against a $4 cashback reward, the code wins easily. For a small percentage-off code against a $4 new-customer cashback, the cashback may well be the better side of the trade.

Do the arithmetic per order instead of defaulting to "always use a code." Run the numbers once and the pattern becomes obvious: big first-order codes beat cashback, small codes often don't.

## A routine that actually works

For a new customer chasing maximum value on one order:

1. Create the ShopBack account and set your payout method to PayPal or ACH.
2. Find a legitimate partner code — the McDonald's page is the model here, since it states the minimum spend, the expiry and the one-per-customer limit.
3. Decide which is worth more: the promo code or the $4 cashback. Pick one.
4. Click through from ShopBack with an empty Uber Eats cart, no ad blocker, on the device you'll order from.
5. Place the order within 2 hours, in the same session.
6. Check your pending cashback, or your promo code's effect on the receipt, within a few days.

For an existing customer ordering regularly, the routine simplifies to one habit: fresh click-through before every order, same device, same session. That single step removes most of the failure modes, and the $0.25 per order accumulates without you thinking about it.

## Quick answers

**Can I use two Uber Eats promo codes on one order?** No. One code per order, and leftover value from a fixed-amount code is lost. Uber Cash and other discounts can still apply.

**Where do existing users find codes?** In your own account — Uber's FAQ points to available promotions listed on your account, plus restaurant-level discounts on the home screen and favourites.

**Are code-aggregator listings trustworthy?** Partly. They're timestamped reports from other shoppers. Most are referral-style codes restricted to first-time users in specific regions, so expect a low hit rate.

**Does cashback stack with Uber One?** Yes, on eligible orders. Uber One membership purchases themselves are excluded.

**Why is the Uber Eats cashback a flat dollar amount?** Because delivery platforms pay per-order commissions rather than a percentage of the basket. The upside for you: a $60 order and a $20 order earn the same reward.

**How long until cashback is usable?** Pending in roughly 48 hours, confirmed after about 45 days for Uber Eats.

None of these numbers are permanent. Rates get boosted during campaigns, codes expire, and bonus thresholds change without notice. Check the live pages before you build an order around any single figure — starting with the cashback rate you'd actually be earning.
